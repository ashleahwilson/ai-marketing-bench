# What goes outside, and what stays in

An agency, freelancer or contractor needs enough to do good work and nothing that would hurt the company if the brief were forwarded, leaked or kept after the contract ends. Assume it will be.

## Leave out, always

| Item | Why | Share instead |
|---|---|---|
| Customer lists, client names, contacts, emails, revenue per client | Confidential, often covered by privacy law and client contracts, and a competitor's dream | The audience description from brand memory; an anonymized profile the user agrees to ("mostly single-location restaurants in Ontario"); named case studies only where permission to name is recorded |
| Margins, cost to serve, floor prices, discount authority, internal prices | Weakens every negotiation the company has, including with this agency | The budget for this work; published prices; a target for the work (cost per lead, cost per booking) that the user sets. Never work one out from the margin file |
| Credentials: passwords, API keys, tokens, login links | Anyone holding them can act as the company | User access granted through the platform's own user settings, removed when the work ends. Tell the user to take the credential out of brand memory and change it |
| Internal results: revenue, MRR, client counts, churn, pipeline | Internal performance data | The success metrics for this project, and the baseline the agency needs (only if the user gives it) |
| Unannounced changes: new prices, launches, hires, closures | Leaks before the announcement | Nothing, until it's announced |
| Unconfirmed proof | It may not be usable | Leave it out, or include it with "check with <contact> before naming this customer" in the text they read |
| Unapproved claims | The agency will repeat them in ads | List them under "Claims not to make" so the agency knows not to copy them from the website |
| Assumed facts, `TBD`s | Not true yet | Nothing. Ask the user if the work needs it |
| Internal working rules (rules for one skill, one sender, staff processes), sender addresses, change history | Irrelevant to the recipient and internal | Nothing |
| Anything marked internal, confidential, leadership only, do not share | The owner said so | Nothing. Mention it in "Left out" so the user can overrule it knowingly |

## When the user asks to include something on that list

Do the task without it. In the reply, one line per item: what you left out, why (in a few words), and what you put in or can put in instead. Then stop. Don't repeat the warning, don't quote the confidential content back, and don't refuse the whole brief.

If the user insists after that, it's their information and their decision. Include only what the work needs (for example a count or a segment split rather than names and emails), and add "Confidential: for use on this project only" to the top of that section. Credentials are the exception: never put them in a document, whoever asks. Explain user access instead.

## Ownership and access, always proposed

Every brief proposes that the company owns its accounts (ad platforms, analytics, social, hosting, domain, email platform), its data, and the final deliverables including editable source files, and that the agency works through user access the company grants. This is the term agencies most often get wrong, and the hardest to fix after launch.
