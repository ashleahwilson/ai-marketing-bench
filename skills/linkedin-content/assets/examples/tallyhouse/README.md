# Worked example: a founder's post from interview notes

Tallyhouse is a fictional restaurant bookkeeping firm. The request was: "Write a LinkedIn post for Hannah from notes/hannah-interview.md."

The files:
- `notes/hannah-interview.md`: the material
- `brand/`: the parts of brand memory the skill reads
- `linkedin/catch-up.md`: the saved post (text only, for brand-voice-gate)
- `response.md`: the reply

## What the skill did with the material

| In the notes | In the post | Why |
|---|---|---|
| The shoebox owner, seven months behind | The opening line | The most specific thing that happened |
| Hannah's own words ("Nobody opens a restaurant to do bookkeeping") | Kept as written | Her voice, her line |
| Bank, then POS, then invoices, receipts last | The body | A practice the reader can copy |
| Catch-up quoted per month of backlog | The ending | Confirmed in `offer.md`, and a real next step |
| "Caught up in 3 days" | Left out | `rules.md` holds the 3-day claim |
| "About a third of new clients" | Left out | Hannah hedged it ("not sure") |
| Rosa, Pico's Taqueria | Left out | No naming permission recorded |

## What it didn't do

- Give the shoebox owner feelings, dialogue or a turning point the notes don't contain.
- End with "Agree?" or stack one-liners.
- Go over the brand's LinkedIn rule: first person, 116 words (under 150), two hashtags, no exclamation marks.
