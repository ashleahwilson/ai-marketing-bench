# Writing the post

How to turn the user's material into a post people read to the end. Everything here works only with facts already on the fact list.

## Find the post in the material

One post makes one point. Before writing, finish this sentence from the material: "The reader should come away knowing ___." If the material holds three points, that's a series or a choice, not one long post.

The strongest material, in order:
1. **Something that happened**, told by the person it happened to, with its real detail (a supplier changed a case size; food cost ran at 38% for five weeks).
2. **A result with its number and its context** (one pilot clinic, 14% to 9%, over 3 months).
3. **A specific practice** the reader can copy (every Monday, sales from the POS, divide, ten minutes).
4. **A point of view** with the reason behind it.

A post with none of these has nothing to say yet. Ask for one, don't decorate the gap.

## The opening

The first line or two show before "see more". They have to earn the click with something specific from the material:
- the moment: "A supplier changed the case size on our chicken and didn't change the invoice."
- the number, in context: "Our pilot clinic's no-show rate went from 14% to 9% in three months."
- the practice: "Every Monday I divide last week's food and labour invoices by sales."
- the claim, stated plainly: "Your POS deposit is not your sales."

Keep the first line under about 150 characters. No population opener ("Most owners…"), no question the reader can answer "no" to, no teaser that withholds the point ("I learned something this week…").

## The body

- Paragraphs of two to four sentences, with varied sentence lengths. A single short line is fine once, for weight. Three in a row is broetry.
- Lists only for things that really are a list (five checklist items). Plain hyphens or numbers, no emoji bullets.
- Tell what happened in the order it happened. Keep the person's own words where the material has them ("Your accountant isn't watching your chicken.").
- Cut the moral if the story already makes it. Never add a lesson the person didn't draw.
- One idea per post. If a second idea shows up, it's the next post.

## The ending

End on one of these, whichever the material supports:
- the practice to try (the plainest, most useful ending)
- the person's own closing line from the material
- a real next step: the offer, the PDF, the review, with a link (in the post or the first comment)
- a question only when the user asks for discussion. A closing question reads as a request for comments, so it's never the default, and in a series at most one post ends on one. Never "Agree?" or "Thoughts?".

## Alternative openings

Give two. Each uses a different fact or angle that's already in the post (the number instead of the moment, the practice instead of the claim). They must work as drop-in replacements for the first line, so they add no new facts and no different promise. The same AI tells apply.

## Links and the first comment

Posting the link in the body or in the first comment is the user's choice. If they say where, do that. If they don't, put it in a suggested first comment and say in one line that they can move it into the post. The first comment is one or two sentences plus the link, and adds no new claim. Never invent a URL: if the post points to something without a link (a PDF, a page), write "message me" or leave a clearly marked `[add link]` in the first comment and list it under "Check before posting".

## Series

- Two to five posts, one idea each, in the order the user gives or the order that builds best.
- Each post stands alone: someone who sees only post 3 understands it. No "In my last post…" unless the user asks.
- Each has a different kind of opening (a moment, a number, a claim), so the series doesn't read as a template.
- Same person, same rules, same limits for every post.
- Save each post to its own file (`linkedin/<slug>-1.md`, `-2.md`…).

## Carousel outline

- A cover slide (the point, under 10 words), five to eight content slides, and a closing slide with the one next step.
- One idea per slide, under 30 words, in plain sentences the reader can scan.
- Number the slides (`## Slide 1`, `## Slide 2`…) and give the exact on-slide text. Add a one-line visual note only if it helps the designer ("screenshot of Settings > Integrations"). Never describe an image you'd have to invent data for (a chart with made-up numbers).
- Then a caption post (`## Caption`) of 40–120 words that makes the point on its own, with the same rules as any post.
- Save it to `linkedin/<slug>-carousel.md`.

## Personal posts and company pages

- **A named person's post** is in their first person ("I"), using only details about them from brand memory or the user. If the channel rule says who writes (for example "first person, by a founder"), follow it. Don't invent their feelings, their past, or a moment that isn't in the material.
- **A company page post** speaks as "we". It can quote people only with their recorded words and permission.
- If the user asks for a company page post and the brand's LinkedIn rule says posts come from a founder, write the company post as asked and mention the rule in one line.

## Defaults when brand memory has no LinkedIn rule

Use these and say the voice is provisional:
- 80–250 words for a text post (LinkedIn allows 3,000 characters; few posts need half that).
- At most three hashtags, at the end, specific to the topic. Zero is fine.
- At most one exclamation mark.
- Keep the writer's own spelling convention from their material.
