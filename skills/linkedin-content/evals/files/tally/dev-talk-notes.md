# Notes: my talk at the Ontario Restaurant Operators breakfast, Oct 16

Who was there: about 40 owners, mostly single locations.

The point I made: check prime cost every week, not at month end. By the time the month-end P&L shows food cost drifted, you've already paid for four weeks of it.

The story I told: when I was controller at a four-location group, a supplier changed the case size on our chicken without changing the invoice line. Food cost ran at 38% for five weeks before anyone noticed. We caught it from the month-end P&L. A weekly prime cost check would have caught it in week one.

What I told them to do: every Monday, pull last week's sales from the POS, add up food and labour invoices, and divide. Ten minutes with a spreadsheet. If it moves more than two points, look at the invoices first.

Someone asked about Tallyhouse: I said we close the books in 3 days, not 3 weeks, and most clients see their P&L by the 3rd.

Line that got a laugh: "Your accountant isn't watching your chicken."
