## Why depth order matters

Generated text gives itself away at five depths. Fix the deepest first. Swapping words in a paragraph that claims nothing produces well-dressed emptiness, which is harder to spot and worse.

| Layer | What's wrong | How long the tell lasts |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Claims | Nothing specific is said | Permanent |
| 2. Moves | The rhetoric is borrowed, not earned | Years |
| 3. Shape | Every unit has the same structure | Years |
| 4. Sentences | Plain construction is avoided | A few years |
| 5. Words | The statistically likely word every time | Changes yearly |

## The specificity gate

Count, per 100 words:
- **named things:** people, companies, products, places, tools
- **numbers:** prices, dates, durations, counts
- **consequences:** something that happened because of something else

Under two in total, the draft has no content, and polishing it is cosmetic. You have two honest options:
- **Cut it shorter,** down to what's real. If `brand/` has relevant confirmed facts (the team, the method, the price), build the short version from those.
- **Ask** for what's missing, in one sentence naming each fact you need.

The forbidden option is inventing the specifics. A made-up customer, number or anecdote reads beautifully and is false. A clearly marked hypothetical ("say a three-location group") is honest.

## Layer 1: is anything being claimed?

- **The swap test.** Put a competitor's name in the sentence. If it still works, it says nothing about this business. Make it specific or delete it.
- **The deletion test.** Cut the sentence. If nothing is lost, leave it cut.
- **The falsifiability test.** Could the sentence be wrong? "We're committed to excellence" can't be, so it can't be informative either.

## Layer 2: borrowed moves

- **Significance clauses:** a fact followed by an "-ing" clause announcing why it matters ("…, highlighting the need for…", "…, empowering you to…"). Keep the fact, cut the clause.
- **"Not X, it's Y" / "not just X, but Y":** say Y.
- **Groups of three:** three adjectives, three benefits, three examples, over and over. Use the real count.
- **Vague authority:** "studies show", "experts agree", "owners are finding". Name the source or drop it.
- **The hedging seesaw:** every claim balanced by its opposite. Commit.
- **Staged questions:** "The result?", "Why does this matter?". Answer it directly.

## Layer 3: shape

- **Uniform sentence length.** Three sentences in a row of about the same length. Vary them.
- **Identical paragraphs.** Claim, elaboration, example, transition, repeated.
- **Intro that previews, ending that recaps.** Start where the content starts; stop when it's done.
- **Lists that should be sentences.** If it fits in one sentence, write the sentence. Lists earn their place in reference material and pricing tables.
- **Transitions doing no work:** "Additionally", "Furthermore", "That said". Delete them.

## Layer 4: sentences

- **Copula avoidance:** "serves as", "stands as", "boasts", "features". Use "is", "has".
- **Passive by default:** name who did it.
- **Nominalisations:** "the implementation of" → "we implemented".
- **Adverb padding:** "significantly", "effectively", "essentially", "seamlessly". Cut and reread.

## Layer 5: words

Judge clusters, not single words. One "robust" in 800 words is nothing. Four of these in one paragraph is a signal: delve, landscape, realm, testament, pivotal, crucial, seamless, robust, leverage, unlock, elevate, empower, foster, transform, game-changer, cutting-edge, holistic, navigate, journey, harness, "in today's fast-paced world", "what matters most", "we've got you covered", "partner in your success".

Two rules matter more than the list:
- **Don't swap slop for slop.** "Tapestry" to "mosaic" fixes nothing. Rewrite the thought.
- **A brand's own word wins.** If `brand/` uses a listed word as a product name or a defined term (a feature called "Seamless Sync"), keep it.

## The overcorrection guard

Heavy-handed clean-up produces its own tells. Undo any of these in the revision:
- Fragments everywhere. For emphasis. Like this.
- Everything chopped to eight words. Uniformly short is still uniform.
- One sentence per paragraph, stacked (broetry).
- Contrarian cold opens: "Everyone tells you X. They're wrong."
- Performed candour: "Honestly?", "Look,", "Let's be real".
- Invented friction or mess the author didn't supply.
- Fake-casual numbers: "like 40-ish percent".
- Assumed intimacy: "You know the feeling."

The final test: **would this sender say it out loud to one reader, without performing?**

## Register by channel

Brand channel rules in `brand/voice.md` override these defaults.

| Channel | Loosen | Tighten |
|---|---|---|
| Email, DM | Brevity, short paragraphs, contractions | No markdown, no greeting or sign-off theatre ("I hope this finds you well", "Looking forward to hearing from you!"), no exclamation marks in a first touch |
| LinkedIn, social | First person, opinion, varied rhythm | No broetry, no hashtag piles, no engagement bait ("Agree?"), no emoji bullets |
| Website, ads | Short sections, second person, one idea per block | No significance language, no unsupported superlatives, every claim checkable |
| Proposals, client documents | Nothing | Full sentences, no fragments for effect, no rhetorical questions; dates, deliverables and amounts stated exactly |
