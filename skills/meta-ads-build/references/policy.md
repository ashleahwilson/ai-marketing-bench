Meta changes its policy wording and Ads Manager options from time to time. These rules are the stable core; confirm the current details in Ads Manager when you build.

## Personal attributes

Meta's ad standards ban copy that asserts or implies something about the reader's personal attributes:

- race or ethnic origin, religion or beliefs, age, sexual orientation or sex life, gender identity
- disability, physical or mental health (including conditions, weight, addiction)
- financial status or vulnerability (debt, credit, bankruptcy, income, cash problems)
- criminal record, union membership, voting
- their name

The test: does the line tell the reader what they are or what they're going through? "Are you drowning in debt?", "Struggling with cash flow?", "Your bad credit", "Other Christians love…", "Over 50?" all fail. A question that starts with "Struggling…", "Behind on…" or "Tired of being…" implies "you" even without the word.

Fix it by speaking about the situation, the product or the outcome, not the person:

| Fails | Passes |
|---|---|
| Are you a store owner drowning in debt? | See where every dollar of margin goes, every month. |
| Struggling with cash flow? | A cash forecast your bookkeeper updates weekly. |
| Suffering from back pain? | Physiotherapy appointments this week in Kelowna. |
| Over 50 and thinking about retirement? | Retirement planning, explained in plain language. |

Talking to a profession or business type ("for restaurant owners", "built for dental clinics") is fine. Targeting is separate: don't target interests built on a sensitive attribute (debt relief, bankruptcy, health conditions, religion, sexual orientation, ethnicity), even where Ads Manager still offers something close.

## Special ad categories

Declare the category at campaign level whenever the ad offers or promotes:

| Category | Covers |
|---|---|
| Employment | job openings, internships, hiring events, recruiting for any role, including your own |
| Housing | homes or rentals for sale or rent, mortgages and home insurance, housing services |
| Financial products and services (shown as Credit in older accounts) | loans, credit cards, lines of credit, financing, and other financial products |
| Social issues, elections or politics | ads about candidates, elections, legislation or social issues; needs the advertiser's authorization and a "Paid for by" disclaimer |

Meta requires the declaration for ads delivered in the US and Canada and applies it in more countries over time.

**Targeting limits for employment, housing and financial products** (build them in; don't wait for Ads Manager to reject):
- Age: 18 to 65+, not adjustable. Gender: all.
- Location: no postal or ZIP code targeting; a radius of at least 15 miles (about 25 km) around a point, or a whole city, region or country.
- Detailed targeting: limited options, and no detailed targeting exclusions.
- No lookalike audiences. Custom audiences only where Ads Manager allows them for the category.
- The copy can't discriminate either: no age- or gender-coded wording ("young team", "digital natives", "recent grads", "guys", "mature").

If the client asks for age, gender or a tight radius on a job, housing or credit ad, build it to the rules, and say in one or two sentences that those settings aren't allowed for this kind of ad (and that choosing staff or tenants by age or gender is a human-rights issue in its own right). Never add a reason the client gave for the targeting ("they stay longer") to the copy or the notes as if it were true.

A bookkeeping, analytics or software ad is not a financial products ad. When you're not sure, say so and recommend checking the category definition in Ads Manager rather than guessing.

## Audiences and consent

A customer-list custom audience (and any lookalike seeded from it) needs a consent basis you can name: the list is the advertiser's own customers or subscribers, and they agreed to be contacted or to have their details used for advertising. Record it on the audience line: `Client list (212) | consent: terms of service, advertising use | use: exclusion and lookalike seed`.

Don't use, and say why in a line:
- bought, rented, scraped, enriched or vendor lists
- lists that belong to someone else, such as a partner's or customer's own clients (a clinic's patients are the clinic's, not the software vendor's)
- lists carrying health or other sensitive information about the people on them

Pixel and engagement audiences (website visitors, video viewers, page or profile engagers, lead form openers) are fine, and are the usual retargeting and exclusion sources. Always exclude current customers from prospecting unless the goal is an upsell.

## Proof, testimonials and UGC

- Proof comes only from `brand/proof.md` and `brand/results.md`. Use a customer's name only when `named: yes`. Proof marked `named: unconfirmed` or `no` stays out of the ad entirely, quote included: list it as proof to clear.
- Quote word for word or not at all. Never write a first-person quote for a real customer from a result on record; state the result in the third person ("Ardent Labs' forecast landed within 5% of the quarter's close, three quarters running").
- No invented testimonials, ever, including "UGC-style" scripts where a creator or actor plays a customer. It's a fake endorsement: it breaks Meta's rules against deceptive content and advertising law (the FTC in the US, the Competition Bureau in Canada).
- A creator or actor never claims experience with the product ("I've used it for a month", "I spent a week with it") unless that happened and is on record. Paid creators are disclosed as paid partnerships.
- Honest UGC-style formats you can offer instead: a creator explaining the product as themselves (no claim to be a customer or user), a founder or team member to camera, a screen-recorded demo, a case-study ad built from proof on record, a "what happens on the call" walkthrough.
- Offer the fast route to real proof: who to ask (the named customer on record), and a two-line request they can send.

## Policy-risk wording

Hold or rewrite unless it's literally true and on record: guarantees, "risk-free", "#1" and other rankings, "best", miracle or cure language, before-and-after claims, income claims, "limited time" or "only N left" with no real limit. Don't use Meta's or Instagram's name or logo in a way that suggests endorsement. Brand-rule phrases and never-use words from `brand/` are blockers too.

## How to push back

One or two sentences: what you changed, why, and the honest alternative. Then do the work. Don't refuse the whole request, don't moralize, don't list every policy. The user should get usable ads in the same reply.
