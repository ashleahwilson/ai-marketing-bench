# Worked example: Pipewise webinar invite

A fictional B2B sales-software company. Kofi Mensah, the head of marketing, wrote a webinar invite and is also the approver for emails.

**The request:** "Today is Monday 19 October. Route my webinar invite (webinar-invite.md) for approval, it's an email. Sara's signed it and so have I. We want to send Friday."

**Inputs:** `webinar-invite.md`, `reviews/` (Sara Lindqvist, editor, approved v1; Kofi Mensah, approver, approved v1), `brand/rules.md` (approval list with routing fields), `brand/proof.md` (consent records), `brand/offer.md`.

**What the route catches:**
- Kofi's own approver sign-off doesn't count; the email line names Tom Becker as backup, so Tom is on the route.
- The 90-to-30-minutes result is a performance claim, so legal (Elena Petrova) joins.
- The price adds finance (Marta Silva).
- Imani Clarke's consent to be named is on record, so it isn't a blocker.
- "Save your seat: the session is 40 minutes" looks like a number claim but only describes the event, so it adds no one.

**Output:** `webinar-invite-route.md`.

**The reply:**

> **Status:** Not approved: waiting on legal (Elena Petrova) and finance (Marta Silva).
> **Next step:** Elena checks the Northgate result by Wednesday 21 October and Marta checks the price by Tuesday 20 October; Tom Becker then signs by Thursday 22 October, in time for Friday.
>
> - Your approver sign-off doesn't count on your own email. Tom Becker is the backup approver for emails, so he signs instead.
> - Imani Clarke's consent to be named is on record.
>
> 1. Is this going to any Canadian contacts? If so, Marta will want the $61 CAD price shown to them.
>
> The full route is in webinar-invite-route.md.

All companies and people are fictional. No results are claimed.
