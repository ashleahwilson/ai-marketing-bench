# Compliance for replies

Practical guidance, not legal advice. When the stakes are high, tell the user to confirm with counsel or the regulator's own guidance. Apply a country's rules by where the recipient is.

## Opt-outs

| Recipient in | Honour within | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Canada (CASL) | 10 business days at most; do it now | A message sent after the request that promotes anything is a new commercial message to someone who withdrew consent. A plain confirmation with no promotion is fine. |
| United States (CAN-SPAM) | 10 business days | Don't sell or transfer the address. |
| UK and EU (PECR, GDPR) | promptly | They may also object to processing; keep only what's needed to suppress them. |
| Australia (Spam Act) | 5 working days | |

Suppress across every sequence, list and sender, not just the campaign they replied to. Keep the suppression row: deleting it is how people get emailed again.

## Hostile replies and complaints

- Don't argue that the email was legal. Suppress, and if a reply helps, give the source on record and confirm removal.
- If they ask where the address came from: in the EU and UK they have a right to know the source of their data; in Canada, being able to show the address was conspicuously published is the sender's defence under CASL's implied consent. Either way, the ledger's source line is the answer. If the ledger has no source, say so to the user and draft nothing about it.
- Tell the user: keep the record of where and when the address was captured, and check the rest of the batch from the same source.

## Referrals

A prospect saying "talk to Victor" tells you who to write to. It doesn't give you consent to write to him.

- **Canada:** CASL's referral exemption covers one message only when the referrer has an existing business, personal or family relationship with the sender *and* with the recipient, and the message names the referrer. A cold prospect usually has no relationship with the sender, so the exemption rarely applies. The new address needs its own basis: typically, that it's conspicuously published by the person or their employer, with no "no unsolicited email" notice, and the message relates to their role. An address handed over in a reply is not published. Flag: "Confirm Victor's address is published (for example on the company site) before sending."
- **US:** no consent needed; the full footer and an opt-out still apply.
- **UK and EU:** treat the new person as a new cold contact under the usual basis.

## Out-of-office colleagues

An address in an auto-reply was given for urgent matters, not published for outreach. In Canada it isn't conspicuously published by the person, so there's no implied consent basis on that alone. Recommend waiting for the original contact.

## Footers

- Replies in the same thread to someone who wrote to you: a normal signature is enough. In Canada, a message sent in response to their request or inquiry is exempt from CASL's consent and form rules; keep it to answering them plus one ask. In the US, if the reply is mainly a sales pitch rather than an answer, CAN-SPAM's postal address and opt-out apply: answer first and keep any offer to one line.
- A first email to a referred person is cold: the sender's name, mailing address and an opt-out line, from `senders.md`. Never write a plausible address; use `[mailing address: needed before sending]` if it's missing.
