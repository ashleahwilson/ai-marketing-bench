# What prime cost is and how to track it weekly

Prime cost is your cost of goods sold plus your total labour cost. For most full-service restaurants it is the biggest number on the P&L, and it is the one you can do the most about in a given week.

## How to calculate prime cost

Add your food and beverage purchases for the period, adjusted for inventory, to your total labour cost: wages, salaries, payroll taxes and benefits. Divide by total sales to get prime cost as a percentage.

Prime cost = (cost of goods sold + total labour) / total sales

## Why weekly beats monthly

If you only see prime cost when the month closes, you find out about a problem three or four weeks after it started. A weekly number lets you change the schedule or the order before the month is gone. You do not need perfect accuracy each week. You need the same method every week so the trend means something.

## What to track each week

- Food and beverage purchases, by supplier
- A quick count of your ten most expensive items
- Labour hours and labour cost from your scheduling or payroll tool
- Net sales from your POS

## Where the time goes

The hard part is not the formula. It is pulling sales from the POS, matching supplier invoices and getting labour numbers out of payroll. That is the work your bookkeeper does for you every month, and it is why owners like Maria Okafor at Lantern Noodle Bar say: "We finally know our prime cost before the month is over."

## Setting a target

Your target depends on your concept, your rent and your menu. Start by tracking it for eight weeks, then set a target you can defend from your own numbers rather than a number from someone else's restaurant.
