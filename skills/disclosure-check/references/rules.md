# The rules, by trigger and country

Name rules at this level in the report: the rule's name, never a section number you aren't sure of, and never a legal conclusion ("this is illegal"). Say what the rule expects and what the piece does instead. Brand rules in `brand/rules.md` sit on top of these: a house rule can be stricter, never looser.

## Which country's rules

Read the countries from `brand/` (`BRAND.md`, `audiences.md`, `senders.md`). If the brand sells or sends to both, check against both and use the stricter requirement. With no brand memory, check against both Canada and the US, say so, mark the result provisional, and ask where they sell. Quebec has its own consumer-protection and contest requirements (including French-language rules): when a piece is open to Quebec residents, say it needs a Quebec check rather than guessing the details.

## Endorsements, sponsored content and creators

| Canada | US |
|---|---|
| Competition Act (misleading representations, including undisclosed material connections); Ad Standards' Influencer Marketing Disclosure Guidelines; the Canadian Code of Advertising Standards | FTC Guides Concerning the Use of Endorsements and Testimonials in Advertising (the Endorsement Guides); FTC "Disclosures 101 for Social Media Influencers" |

Both expect any material connection (payment, free product, commission, discount code, employment, family, investment) to be disclosed clearly, close to the endorsement, in words the audience understands. Clear terms: "Ad", "#ad", "Sponsored", "#sponsored", "Paid partnership with @brand". Not clear on their own: "#sp", "#spon", "#collab", "#partner", "#ambassador", "thanks to @brand", a disclosure only in the profile bio, or only on the platform's label. The platform's paid-partnership label helps and should be on, but it doesn't replace the words in the caption. The brand is responsible for what its paid creators claim: a creator's claim the brand couldn't make itself (an unsubstantiated result, a claim brand rules bar) is a problem in the creator's post too.

## Testimonials and reviews with a connection

| Canada | US |
|---|---|
| Competition Act (misleading representations; testimonials); Canadian Code of Advertising Standards (testimonials must be genuine, current, and reflect the person's real experience) | Endorsement Guides; the FTC rule on consumer reviews and testimonials (insider reviews without disclosure, fake or AI-generated testimonials) |

An employee, founder, investor, family member, or a customer given something in exchange (free months, a discount, a gift card, entry into a draw) is a material connection. Disclose it next to the quote, in the attribution line. A job title or company name that hides the connection ("owner, Saffron Room" for a current employee) is itself misleading: correct the attribution, don't just add a footnote. A quote from someone who doesn't exist, or a generated person presented as a customer, can't be fixed with a disclosure: remove it.

## Affiliate links

Same rules as endorsements (both countries). Disclose before the first affiliate link, near the top, in plain words ("We earn a commission if you buy through some links in this post"). A line at the end of the post, a separate disclosure page, or "affiliate links may be present" in the footer is too late. Use the brand's own wording from `brand/rules.md` word for word when it exists.

## Prices and fees

| Canada | US |
|---|---|
| Competition Act: misleading price representations, and drip pricing (a price that can't be attained because mandatory fees are added later) | FTC Act Section 5 (deceptive pricing); state laws on hidden or junk fees |

A "from" price needs its conditions beside it: billing period (billed annually), minimums (5 users), what's excluded. A mandatory fee (setup, onboarding, service) belongs in the price or immediately beside it, not only in an FAQ, footnote or checkout. Taxes and genuinely optional add-ons can be stated separately.

## Free trials and automatic renewal

| Canada | US |
|---|---|
| Competition Act (misleading representations, drip pricing); provincial consumer-protection laws on internet and continuing agreements | FTC Negative Option Rule and ROSCA (Restore Online Shoppers' Confidence Act); state automatic-renewal laws. The FTC's 2024 "click to cancel" amendments were vacated in 2025, so ROSCA and state laws carry the detail |

Before the sign-up button, state: that it renews automatically, the price after the trial (and billing period), when the first charge happens, and how to cancel. "No strings", "no commitment" or "risk-free" next to a card requirement and automatic billing contradicts the terms.

## Contests and giveaways

| Canada | US |
|---|---|
| Competition Act (contest disclosure: number and value of prizes, chances of winning, regional allocation); Criminal Code lottery provisions (why Canadian contests require a skill-testing question and no purchase); Quebec's contest rules for entrants there | State lottery laws (prize + chance + required purchase = illegal lottery, so "no purchase necessary"); official rules; some states require registration or bonding for large prize pools |

The post itself needs: no purchase necessary, who can enter, the closing date and time (with time zone), and a link to the full rules. The rules cover: eligibility, how to enter (including a no-purchase method), how and when the winner is chosen, the odds ("depend on the number of eligible entries"), the prize and its approximate value, the skill-testing question for Canadian winners, and any regional exclusions. On Instagram or Facebook, the platform's promotion terms require a release of the platform and a statement that it isn't sponsored, endorsed or administered by the platform. Recommend a lawyer reviews the full rules, in one sentence.

## Commercial email

| Canada | US |
|---|---|
| CASL (Canada's Anti-Spam Legislation): consent, sender identification, contact information including a mailing address, and an unsubscribe mechanism that works for 60 days and is honoured within 10 business days | CAN-SPAM Act: accurate From and subject line, a valid physical postal address, an opt-out mechanism honoured within 10 business days, and identification as an ad unless the recipient opted in |

A subject line that starts "Re:" or "Fwd:" when there was no earlier conversation is misleading under both. Use the mailing address and opt-out wording from `brand/senders.md` when they exist; never invent an address or an unsubscribe URL (the email platform supplies the link).

## AI-generated or altered imagery

There is no general Canadian or US rule that every AI image in an ad is labelled. Two things still apply:
1. **Brand rules.** If `brand/rules.md` requires a label, use its exact wording and placement.
2. **Misleading impressions** (Competition Act; FTC Act). A generated or altered image that shows a "customer", a result or a product feature as real is misleading whatever the label. Platforms may also apply their own AI labels.

## What "clear and where they'll see it" means

- **Social captions:** in the first line, before the "more" cut-off (about 125 characters on Instagram, less on some layouts), not in the hashtag block, not only in comments or the bio. On video, also said or shown in the video.
- **Web pages and posts:** above or beside the claim or link it qualifies, readable size and contrast, not behind a hover, tooltip or "see terms" link.
- **Email:** with the content it qualifies; identification and unsubscribe in the footer is fine.
- **Ads:** in the ad itself (image or primary text), not only on the landing page.
