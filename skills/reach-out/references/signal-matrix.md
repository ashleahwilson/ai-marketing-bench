# Signal matrix

The signal is whatever made this prospect worth writing to today. It decides five things before any copy exists. Find the row, apply it, then read that signal's notes.

## Contents

## The matrix

| Signal | Can the copy name it? | Usable for | Write to | Ask | Default touches |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Named account** (the user chose it) | Name the public fact that makes the problem likely, not the reason the user chose them | Until the fact goes stale | Whoever owns the problem day to day | Interest question or concrete offer | 3 |
| **Company event** (raise, executive hire, launch, expansion, hiring burst) | Only as its consequence, never in the first sentence or subject | About 6 weeks | Owner of the consequence, often not the person in the news | Interest question | 3 |
| **Tool adoption / stack** | As a pattern ("teams in their first year on X"), never as an observation | About 6 months | The person who runs the tool | Concrete offer | 3 |
| **Website visit** | Never | 1–3 days | Owner of the topic they read about | Soft offer | 3 |
| **Third-party intent** | Never, and only act on it with a second signal you can name | About 14 days | Owner of the researched topic | Soft offer | 3 |
| **Their LinkedIn post** | Yes, by engaging with what it argued | About 48 hours | The author | A question about their topic, no pitch | Connect, then DM, then 1 follow-up |
| **Event attendance** | Yes, it's shared context | 48 hours after the event | Depends on how they engaged | Extend the content | 2–3 |
| **Inbound action** (form, demo request, signup, reply to content) | Yes, quote their words | Same hour | The person who acted | Answer, then a direct way to book | 1, plus up to 2 short nudges |
| **Champion moved** (a past user or buyer started a new role) | Yes, the shared history | First 90 days in the role | The champion | A catch-up, not a pitch | 2 |
| **Re-engagement** (closed-lost, went quiet, old opportunity) | Yes, honestly, plus what has changed since | Only with a new reason | The prior contact, or their successor | "Has anything changed?" plus the new reason | 2 |
| **Referral** (someone suggested the contact) | Yes, name the referrer once they've agreed | While the intro is fresh | The person referred | A short call or a direct question | 2 |
| **Reply received** | n/a | Same day | The person who replied | Depends on the reply | See notes |

"Default touches" assumes the user hasn't set a cadence. The profile and the user's request override it.

## Notes per signal

**Named account.** Ask what the user knows that made them pick this account, and use the strongest public fact behind it: a job posting, a public complaint, a stated goal, a migration. One fact is enough. If the only reason is "they fit our ICP", say the hook is persona pain and treat it as the weakest kind.

**Company event.** Write the second-order effect: a raise means a plan that now has to deliver, a new executive means a 90-day agenda, a hiring burst means process strain arriving before the tooling does. Check the event against two sources if you are inferring anything from it. Calibrate: one job post is not a hiring burst, and three roles on one team is.

**Tool adoption / stack.** Technographic data tells you what problem to raise. It is not something to report back. Write "the first quarter-end after a NetSuite move is usually where reconciliations pile up", not "looks like you moved to NetSuite". Use "a" or "after a", never "the", because "the" claims you know their event.

**Website visit and third-party intent.** These tell you timing and topic, nothing more. The email must read as if you would have sent it anyway. If only the company is known, name the likely title or titles that own the topic, so the user knows who to look up. A pricing-page view is not a buying committee.

**Their LinkedIn post.** Respond to the argument they made, the way a peer would: agree with a reason, add a data point, or disagree politely. Send the connection request without a note, or with one under 300 characters that has no pitch. Send the DM after they accept. Days count from acceptance. No product name or proof numbers in the first DM. If they never accept, stop. Don't move to email.

**Event attendance.** Tier by engagement: met in person, asked a question, attended quietly, registered only. Only the first two get anything personal. The sender is the person who was actually there. No-shows get the recording plus the one point worth their time.

**Inbound action.** Speed beats polish. Answer their question in the first line, then give a booking link or two specific times. If the user hasn't given either, use `[booking link]` and flag it. Don't ask qualifying questions the form already answered, don't start a nurture sequence, and stop any cold sequence already running to them. Proof is optional.

**Champion moved.** The relationship is the hook. Congratulate briefly (this is the one place it fits), ask how the new role is going, and save the product for the second message or their reply. Suggesting a catch-up call is fine here, because the relationship already exists. Pull what they valued last time from the CRM or the user.

**Re-engagement.** Say plainly that you spoke before, and give the one new thing: a feature they asked for, a new proof point, a change on their side. Without a new reason, don't send.

**Referral.** Confirm the referrer is happy to be named before using their name. Then lead with it: "[Name] suggested I get in touch about …". Under Canada's anti-spam law (CASL), a referral can support a first message only if the referrer has a relationship with both sides and is named in it. See [send-gate.md](send-gate.md).

**Reply received.** If a reply-handling skill is installed, hand over to it. Otherwise:
- **Unsubscribe or "stop":** no reply. Stop all sequences and record the opt-out.
- **Wrong person:** thank them and ask who owns it.
- **Not now:** agree a date to come back, then go quiet.
- **Objection:** answer it in two sentences, with one piece of evidence.
- **Interested:** answer and give a way to book.

Never treat "not interested" as an unsubscribe, or the reverse.

Record opt-outs in the CRM if one is connected and the user confirms. Otherwise, tell the user exactly what to record.

When one reply is both an opt-out and a pointer ("not me, talk to Sam, and take me off your list"), stop everything to the sender, then write to the new person as a fresh cold message about their role. Draft it, don't hold it. Don't use the opted-out person's name unless they offered an introduction.

## When several signals apply

Use the strongest signal the copy is **allowed to name**: inbound, then referral, then champion moved, then their post, then event attendance, then a company event. Silent signals (visits, intent, stack) are only tie-breakers for timing. Never stack two signals in one email.
