# Worked example: cold email to a Canadian prospect from a data provider

Fictional companies and people. No results claimed. This shows the full output shape: send-ready copy first, a short "why", and a compliance hold at the top of the check list.

## Input

> Write a first email to Martin Gagnon, Director of Operations at Boréal Logistique in Montréal (about 220 employees). I got his email from a ZoomInfo export. We sell Dockwise, dock-scheduling software for 3PL warehouses. Proof: Fraser Cold Storage (150 people) cut truck wait times from 2 hours to 25 minutes. I'm Priya Nair, AE at Dockwise, 401 Bay St, Toronto.

## Output

**Hold before sending:** Martin is in Canada, and an address from a ZoomInfo export doesn't give you implied consent under CASL (see Check before sending). The draft is ready for when you have a basis.

**Send this one**

Subject: dock wait times

Hi Martin,

When carriers wait for a door, an operation the size of Boréal Logistique usually pays twice: detention charges, and a dock crew that's idle, then slammed.

Fraser Cold Storage (150 people) cut truck wait times from 2 hours to 25 minutes after moving dock scheduling to Dockwise.

Open to a look at how they set it up?

Priya Nair, Account Executive, Dockwise
401 Bay St, Toronto
If this isn't relevant to you, reply "no" and I won't follow up.

**Why**
- Recipient: Martin Gagnon, Director of Operations, who owns dock throughput day to day. The angle is cost and crew time, pitched at a director.
- Signal: named account (you, 2026-10-10), with no public fact behind it, so the hook is persona pain. That's the weakest kind. One fact would make it much stronger: a Boréal job post for dock or yard staff, a stated expansion, or a carrier complaint about wait times.
- Proof: Fraser at 150 people sits close to Boréal's 220. Voice is provisional, since there's no outreach profile yet.

**Alternative subject:** carrier wait at Boréal

**Check before sending**
1. **CASL consent (Canada).** Implied consent needs an address Martin or Boréal published conspicuously, such as on Boréal's website, with no "no unsolicited messages" notice. A ZoomInfo export doesn't count, and you'd need to be able to prove the basis. Options: find his address published on Boréal's site and record where, get a referral from someone who knows you both (named in the email), or connect on LinkedIn first. This is practical guidance, not legal advice.
2. **Fraser Cold Storage named.** Confirm they've agreed to be named. If not, use "a 150-person cold storage operator". Also confirm the 2 hours to 25 minutes came from Dockwise, as the email says, and that you can show "how they set it up" if he replies.
3. **Language and fit.** Boréal is Montréal-based, so check whether Martin works in French; a French version may land better. I've also assumed Boréal runs 3PL docks, which you didn't state. Your signature has the mailing address CASL requires, and your reply address covers the contact method.

**Checked:** length, subject, sources, opt-out, signature. All clear.

Want follow-ups 2 and 3 for the same thread? And so the next draft starts with your details, three quick questions to set up your profile:
1. Is Priya Nair, Account Executive, Dockwise, 401 Bay St, Toronto the sender for all outreach, and should the signature carry a website or phone number too?
2. In one line: what does Dockwise sell, and to whom (3PL warehouses of what size)?
3. Has Fraser Cold Storage agreed to be named, and do you have other results?
