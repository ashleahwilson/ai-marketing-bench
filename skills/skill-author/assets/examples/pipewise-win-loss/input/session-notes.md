# Win-loss summaries: three sessions
Rosa Lindqvist (Pipewise product marketing) with an AI assistant. Pipewise sells B2B sales forecasting software.
Source: interview transcripts with buyers who chose or rejected Pipewise. The real buyer companies and
interviewees have been replaced here with fictional stand-ins.

## Session 1, 2026-09-08 (a loss: Northgate Freight, VP Sales)

Assistant's draft opened: "Northgate chose a competitor mainly because of price."
Rosa: "No. She said price came up once. The reason she gave three times was that our setup needed their
RevOps person for six weeks and they don't have one. Lead with the reason the buyer gave most often,
and say how many times they gave it."

Draft quoted: "Pipewise was too expensive and too complicated."
Rosa: "That's a paraphrase in quote marks. Quotes are word for word from the transcript or they're not quotes."

Rosa: "And put the deal size at the top, we always want it. $48,000 a year here." (Deal sizes are in the CRM export.)

## Session 2, 2026-09-22 (a win: Halden Supply, CRO)

Draft had a "Recommendations" section: "Pipewise should cut its price by 15%."
Rosa: "Don't recommend. The summary reports what the buyer said. Product decides what to do with it.
If you want, add 'Questions this raises' at the end, as questions."

Draft was 600 words.
Rosa: "One page. Under 300 words. Sales reads these on their phone."

Rosa: "Use 'buyer', not 'customer', a loss was never a customer."

## Session 3, 2026-10-06 (a loss: Pell & Moss, Head of RevOps)

Draft said: "They will likely revisit in Q2."
Rosa: "Did he say that? He said 'maybe next year'. Don't predict. Write what he said."

Draft named the competitor's sales rep.
Rosa: "Never name individual people at other companies, just the company."

Rosa: "This format is right now. Make it a skill so the whole team writes them this way."
