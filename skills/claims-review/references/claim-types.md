# Claim types: risk, substantiation, qualifiers

## Contents

## What counts as a claim

A claim is anything a reader could check and find false: a number, a result, a ranking, a comparison, a promise, a price, a condition, an endorsement, a certification, or a statement about what the product does or is made of. Judge the general impression as well as the literal words: "Twice as fast" next to a competitor's logo is a comparative claim even if the competitor isn't named.

Not claims: honest opinion that no one could measure ("you'll love Monday mornings again"), a brand's voice lines ("back to the kitchen, out of the spreadsheets"), and an endorser's clearly personal taste ("the only one I still open every morning"). Leave them alone. Treat them as claims only when they imply something measurable ("the most accurate", "saves you hours").

## Risk levels

| Risk | When |
|---|---|
| **High** | Health, medical or safety; environmental; financial outcomes (revenue, ROI, savings, earnings); any guarantee; a comparison with a named or identifiable competitor; "clinically / scientifically proven" or "tests show"; price comparisons, hidden or mandatory fees, "free" with undisclosed conditions; an endorsement with an undisclosed paid or family connection; anything claims.md lists as Restricted or rules.md bans; anything brand memory contradicts; regulated-status claims (HIPAA, certified, approved) |
| **Medium** | A performance number with no source; superlatives and rankings; popularity counts and ratings; capability claims beyond what offer.md says; a testimonial with naming permission unconfirmed or wording that differs from proof.md; absolutes ("always", "never", "every") |
| **Low** | A supported fact missing a qualifier (the plan, the region, the date it was true) |
| **Supported** | Matches an approved claim in claims.md or a confirmed fact in brand memory, used in the same context. No action |

A brand rule or Restricted entry always wins: a claim that rules.md bans is high risk even if it's true.

## Claim types

### Performance and results ("cuts costs 40%", "close in 3 days")
- **Needed:** data that measures exactly what the claim says, gathered before the claim runs: a study, client data across a stated sample, or a test. One customer's result supports only that customer's story, attributed.
- **Missing qualifier:** who, how many, over what period, compared with what ("across 42 clients, January to June 2026").
- **Rewrite:** state the mechanism the brand can prove (what the product does), or a single attributed customer result from proof.md. No number unless brand memory has it.

### Comparative ("faster than LedgerLoop", "40% more accurate than Dealcraft")
- **Needed:** a like-for-like test against that competitor, same conditions, recent, documented. The competitor may challenge it.
- **Missing qualifier:** what was compared, how, when.
- **Rewrite:** drop the competitor and state what's true of the brand alone. Check rules.md for a rule about naming competitors.

### Superlative and ranking ("#1", "the most accurate", "the leading", "the only")
- **Needed:** proof that would hold up if a competitor complained: a ranked, independent source, or a market-share figure. "The only" needs proof that no one else does it.
- **Rewrite:** cut, or replace with a specific, supported fact. Never a softened superlative ("one of the leading", "among the best"): it's still an unsupported ranking.

### Free and pricing ("free", "was $90, now $45", "no hidden fees", "cancel anytime")
- **Needed:** the real terms from offer.md. A "was" price must be a price actually charged for a meaningful period (check the price history). Any mandatory fee must be in the headline price or shown with it. "Free" must disclose every condition (card required, auto-renewal, what it converts to, eligibility).
- **Missing qualifier:** billing period, minimums, currency, mandatory fees, renewal terms, cancellation terms.
- **Rewrite:** state the real price and terms in plain words near the claim.

### Guarantees ("guaranteed", "money back", "or it's free")
- **Needed:** a written guarantee the business will honour, with its terms (what's covered, how to claim, time limit). Check claims.md: many brands restrict guarantees outright.
- **Rewrite:** cut, or state the actual policy from brand memory with its terms.

### Testimonials and endorsements
- **Needed:** the testimonial recorded in proof.md, wording matching, naming permission `yes`. A result in a testimonial is a claim the brand makes: it needs substantiation and must reflect what customers generally get, or say what they generally get. "Results not typical" doesn't cure it.
- **Paid, gifted or connected endorsers** (creators, affiliates, employees, family): the connection must be disclosed clearly and up front, in the post itself, not in a hashtag pile or a bio link.
- **Professional endorsements** ("dentist recommended", "dermatologist recommended") need documented endorsement by those professionals and the basis for it.
- **Rewrite:** use only recorded quotes with permission; anonymize when permission is unconfirmed; add the disclosure.

### Popularity and ratings ("trusted by 500+ teams", "4.9 stars from 2,000 reviews")
- **Needed:** the current figure from brand memory (billing system, review platform), with its date.
- **Rewrite:** use the recorded figure, or cut. Never round up.

### Environmental ("carbon neutral", "biodegradable", "plastic-free", "natural", "eco-friendly")
- **Needed:** product claims need a test of the specific benefit (biodegradability test, material certificate). Business-wide claims ("carbon neutral company") need evidence under a recognized methodology and, for offsets, third-party verification. Vague terms ("eco-friendly", "green", "natural") need a specific, provable meaning or should go.
- **Missing qualifier:** which part (box, wrap, formula), under what conditions, certified by whom.
- **Rewrite:** name the specific, proven fact ("box made from 100% recycled cardboard").

### Health and safety ("soothes eczema", "gentle enough for babies", "clinically proven")
- **Needed:** competent scientific evidence for the specific effect, at the level the claim implies ("clinically proven" means clinical trials). Claims to treat or relieve a condition can change how a product is regulated. Always high risk; always legal or regulatory review.
- **Rewrite:** ingredients and how it's made, from confirmed brand facts. No effect on a condition.

### Financial outcomes ("23% more revenue", "3x ROI", "pays for itself")
- **Needed:** data across a representative group of customers, with method and period. Earnings and ROI claims are scrutinized closely.
- **Rewrite:** what the product does, or one attributed customer quote from proof.md.

### AI and capability ("AI-powered", "predicts every deal", "real-time")
- **Needed:** the product actually does it, as described, today. offer.md is the source. "Predicts every deal with 95% accuracy" is a performance claim on top of a capability claim. Check rules.md: some brands ban "AI-powered" or "real-time".
- **Rewrite:** describe the capability as offer.md does.

### Certification and regulatory status ("HIPAA-compliant", "certified", "approved")
- **Needed:** the certificate, audit or agreement behind it. Some regimes have no official certification at all (HIPAA has none), so the claim needs evidence of the safeguards it implies.
- **Rewrite:** state the specific, documented fact, or cut.

## Writing the rewrite

- Use only words and facts in the piece or in brand memory marked confirmed or approved.
- When nothing supports a version of the claim, the rewrite is **Cut**. Say so plainly.
- Keep the brand's spelling convention (voice.md), not the reviewer's.
- Keep it the same length or shorter, and in the brand's voice.
- No new numbers, customers, certifications, competitors or dates.
