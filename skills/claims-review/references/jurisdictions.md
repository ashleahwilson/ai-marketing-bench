# Rules by market

Name the rule so the legal reviewer can check faster. Never interpret it beyond this table, never say a claim "complies", and never give a penalty estimate. Rules change: say "as recorded here" and let the reviewer confirm current wording.

## Choosing the market

1. The user names one in the request: use it.
2. Brand memory names one (BRAND.md "where we sell / market", audiences.md segments, senders.md countries, a `Markets:` line in claims.md): use it, and say where it came from.
3. Neither: say the market isn't recorded, ask which markets the piece runs in, and meanwhile apply the principles both markets share (claims need evidence before they run; the overall impression counts, not just the literal words; material terms and paid connections must be clear). Mark the review provisional. Don't present one country's law as governing.

Apply only the markets in scope. A US-only brand doesn't get Canadian rules, and the reverse. If the piece itself targets a market outside brand memory (prices in CAD for a US-only brand), flag that as a question.

## Canada

| Claim type | Rule to name |
|---|---|
| Any false or misleading claim | Competition Act, s. 74.01(1)(a) (civil) and s. 52 (criminal, knowing or reckless). The general impression counts as well as the literal meaning |
| Performance, efficacy, "proven" | Competition Act, s. 74.01(1)(b): must be based on an adequate and proper test, done before the claim is made |
| Tests and testimonials | Competition Act, s. 74.02: a test or testimonial must actually have been made or given, with permission |
| Environmental, product | Competition Act, s. 74.01(1)(b.1) (2024 amendments): environmental benefit claims need an adequate and proper test |
| Environmental, business-wide ("carbon neutral company") | Competition Act, s. 74.01(1)(b.2) (2024 amendments): needs adequate and substantiated evidence under an internationally recognized methodology. Competition Bureau guidance on environmental claims |
| Guarantees and warranties | Competition Act, s. 74.01(1)(c); Canadian Code of Advertising Standards, clause 5 (Guarantees) |
| "Was" prices, "save X%" | Competition Act, s. 74.01(2) and (3), ordinary selling price; Code clause 3 (Price Claims) |
| Mandatory fees not in the price | Competition Act, s. 74.01(1.1) and s. 52(1.3), drip pricing |
| Comparisons | Code clause 6 (Comparative Advertising) |
| Testimonials | Code clause 7 (Testimonials) |
| Scientific or professional claims | Code clause 8 (Professional or Scientific Claims) |
| Accuracy, fine print, disclaimers | Code clause 1 (Accuracy and Clarity): a disclaimer can't contradict the main claim |
| Influencers and paid endorsements | Competition Bureau guidance on influencer marketing; Ad Standards Influencer Marketing Disclosure Guidelines |
| Health claims on cosmetics, soaps, supplements | Food and Drugs Act; Health Canada guidance on the cosmetic-drug interface: a claim to treat or relieve a condition can make the product a drug or natural health product |
| Quebec audiences | Quebec Consumer Protection Act and French-language requirements: flag for local review |

The Code is administered by Ad Standards; complaints can come from consumers or competitors.

## United States

| Claim type | Rule to name |
|---|---|
| Any deceptive claim | FTC Act, s. 5; FTC Policy Statement on Deception (net impression, reasonable consumer, materiality) |
| Any objective claim | FTC Policy Statement Regarding Advertising Substantiation: a reasonable basis before the claim runs. "Tests show" or "clinically proven" needs the level of proof claimed |
| Health and safety | FTC Act s. 12 (food, drugs, devices, cosmetics); FTC Health Products Compliance Guidance: competent and reliable scientific evidence |
| Endorsements, testimonials, influencers | FTC Endorsement Guides, 16 CFR Part 255: clear and conspicuous disclosure of material connections; an endorser's results imply typical results unless the generally expected result is disclosed |
| Fake or misrepresented reviews | FTC Rule on the Use of Consumer Reviews and Testimonials, 16 CFR Part 465 |
| Environmental | FTC Green Guides, 16 CFR Part 260 (general benefit claims, degradable, compostable, recyclable, carbon offsets) |
| "Was" prices, "save X%" | FTC Guides Against Deceptive Pricing, 16 CFR Part 233 |
| "Free" offers | FTC Guide Concerning Use of the Word "Free", 16 CFR 251 |
| Free trials that convert, auto-renewal | Restore Online Shoppers' Confidence Act (ROSCA); state automatic-renewal laws |
| Comparisons | Lanham Act, s. 43(a) (competitor lawsuits); challenges at the National Advertising Division |
| AI capability claims | FTC Act, s. 5: the FTC has brought cases over unsupported AI claims |
| HIPAA claims | No official HIPAA certification exists; an unsupported "HIPAA-compliant" claim is a deception risk under FTC Act s. 5 |

## Other markets

Outside Canada and the US (UK, EU, Australia), say the review applied general substantiation principles only, name the market, and route to someone qualified there. Don't name rules from memory.
