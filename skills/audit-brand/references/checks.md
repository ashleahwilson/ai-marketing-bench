## Voice consistency

Read every piece, then compare the scan's measures across them. A voice break is a piece that a reader would notice was written by someone else.

| Measure | What a break looks like | Not a break |
|---|---|---|
| Sentence length | One piece averages 50% longer or 40% shorter than the median | Headings, lists and a pricing table reading short |
| Contractions | None on one page while the rest use them freely | A legal or policy page written formally |
| Person | "The company", "Tallyhouse Inc. is committed to…" on a page where every other page says "we" and "you" | A homepage subhead that names the product ("Tallyhouse connects your POS…") |
| Spelling convention | US and Commonwealth forms in the same set ("labor" on pricing, "labour" on home), or -ise beside -ize | One convention used throughout, whichever it is |
| Stock and banned words | A cluster of stock phrases ("best-in-class solutions that empower stakeholders") | A product name that contains a stock word ("Seamless Sync") |
| Register in channels | A post full of "!!!" and hashtags when the others are plain | One exclamation mark where the brand memory allows it |
| Tense | Promises in the future ("will revolutionise") where the rest describes what the product does now | Dated announcements |

Spelling is judged against the company's convention: its brand memory, or else the one most of its pages use. If the pages split evenly or carry few marker words, the market decides: USD prices, a US address or US-only roles mean US spelling. Always name the convention to use in the fix; "pick one" isn't a fix. Name the pages on each side, quote the words, and recommend the company's convention ("use 'labour' on pricing to match the home page and LinkedIn"). Canadian uses -our and -re with -ize; British uses -our and -re with -ise; US uses -or and -er with -ize.

## Message drift

Anything that contradicts the core story, on one page or across pages: an "AI-powered" banner above "a real bookkeeper, not a chatbot"; a product page selling "enterprise-grade revenue intelligence" when every other page sells forecasting to sales teams; a different audience or price on a channel than on the site. Quote both sides.

## Naming and terminology

Check the company name, product and feature names, partner names and the name of the main offer across every piece and page title.
- **A finding:** "TallyHouse" in one title and "Tallyhouse" everywhere else; "Deal Radar", "DealRadar" and "dealradar"; "Month End Review" in one heading and "month-end review" elsewhere; a partner's name misspelled ("LightSpeed" for "Lightspeed").
- **Not a finding:** a capital at the start of a sentence or heading; the domain in an email address.
- **The fix names the form to use:** the brand memory's form if it records one, otherwise the most common form on the site.

## Proof and trust

List every proof item and trust signal with its page.

| Signal | Credible | Weak |
|---|---|---|
| Testimonials | A named person, their role and company, specific words | "VP Sales, SaaS company"; a quote with no name |
| Customer claims | Named customers, or a count with its source | "Trusted by 1,000+ teams worldwide", "hundreds of restaurants" |
| Ratings and reviews | A score with the platform named, ideally linked | "Rated 4.8/5 by our users" with no source |
| Credentials | A named qualification held by a named person (a CPA founder) | "Certified experts" with nothing named |
| Team | Founders or staff named with roles | "Our team" only |
| Contact details | Email, phone or street address on the site | A form only, or nothing |
| Policies | Privacy, terms, refund or cancellation linked | None |

- **Permission.** If the company's brand memory records a quote's naming permission as unconfirmed, the quote on the site is a risk: flag it and say who needs to agree.
- **Unsupported figures.** A figure on a page or post that isn't on the site's own pages or in brand memory ("Our clients save 20 hours a month") is unsupported. Flag it, ask for the source, and suggest cutting it until there is one.
- **Never fill a gap.** If the user asks for a review score, customer count or rating that isn't on the pages supplied, say it isn't observable and what would supply it (the review profile link, the company's own count). Quote what the site claims and label it as the company's unverified claim.

Contact details and policies are reported here, but scored elsewhere in the marketing audit (privacy notices sit in conversion and measurement). Don't take B-points for them.

## Asset care

From the HTML: images with no alt text, a filename as alt text ("IMG_2041.jpg", "image1"), or alt text that names nothing ("customer logos"); page title formats that differ (brand first on one page, last on the others; different separators; the company name spelled differently); placeholder text ("Lorem ipsum", "TBD", "Coming soon"). Say what alt text needs to describe, never a guess at what the image shows. From markdown or copied text, alt text and titles can't be checked: say so.

## The brand memory gap analysis

Only when `BRAND.md` names the audited company. Compare the pages and posts with:
- **voice.md:** principles, stated spelling convention, words we use (including the exact form of product names), words we never use, channel rules (length, exclamation marks, hashtags).
- **rules.md:** every "never" and "always" line, quoted beside the breach.
- **claims.md:** any claim on the site listed under "Needs substantiation" or "Restricted".
- **proof.md:** quotes reproduced exactly, and their naming permission.
- **offer.md:** prices and offer names on the pages match.

Each breach quotes the brand memory line and the page line. Respect each rule's scope: an email rule doesn't apply to LinkedIn.

If the `brand/` folder is someone else's, write one line in the report ("The brand/ folder in this project is Brightpath Advisory's, not Quillmetrics', so it wasn't used") and use none of its rules, words or spelling.

## What can't be judged from words

Always list it in the report: logo design and use, the colour palette, typography, photography and imagery style, and how the brand looks on mobile. If the user asked for a visual rating, say plainly that you can't give one from text and what you'd need to do it. If only markdown or copied text was supplied, add alt text and page titles.

## The brand checklist (B1–B5)

The marketing audit's brand checks, with deeper tests. Score every check full, partial or 0, with one line of evidence. When torn between two levels, award the lower one and say why. Score each issue once.

| ID | Check | Full | Partial | 0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | Voice consistency | 25: no voice breaks; one spelling convention | 12: one piece breaks register, or the spelling mixes, or stock phrases cluster on one page | Two or more pieces clash in register (for example a third-person corporate page and a hype post beside a plain voice) |
| B2 | No message drift | 25: nothing contradicts the core story | 12: minor drift (a secondary page sells a wider or vaguer version) | A visible contradiction, such as an "AI-powered" banner on a brand that sells "a real person, not a chatbot" |
| B3 | Proof credibility | 20: proof is attributed and specific, and no unsupported figures | 10: some vague or unsupported proof beside credible proof | Only vague, unattributed or unsourced proof |
| B4 | Asset care | 20: alt text on every image, one title format, consistent naming, no placeholders | 10: a few gaps | Many gaps, or placeholder text live on a page |
| B5 | Matches brand memory | 10: no breaches of the company's rules, claims, words or stated spelling | 5: a minor breach (one word, one channel rule) | A rule breach, or a claim listed as needing substantiation shown as fact |

- **Where each issue scores:** register and spelling mix in B1; contradictions in B2; testimonials, ratings, customer claims and unsupported figures in B3; alt text, titles, naming forms and placeholders in B4; anything that breaks the company's brand memory also counts in B5, because B5 exists to show that gap.
- **B5 without the company's brand memory** (none exists, or the `brand/` folder is someone else's): 10 if B1 and B2 both scored full, otherwise 5. Set `"brand_memory": "none"` or `"auditor"` in `dim-brand.json`.
- **Markdown or copied text only:** score B4 on naming and placeholders, say alt text and titles weren't observable, and award at most 10.
- **No maturity relief.** None of the brand checks is relaxed for a founder-led company. The plan is sized to the company instead.

## Severity and the top three

`high` for 0 points on a check worth 20 or more, or a breach of a brand-memory rule or claim; `medium` for a partial on a check worth 20 or more, or a minor brand-memory breach; `low` for the rest. Rank by severity, then reach (how many pages and channels), then how fast it can be fixed. Strengths come from checks that scored full and from pieces with no breaks.
