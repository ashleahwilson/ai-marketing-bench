## How to use these defaults

Start from the type's default, then cut. Fewer, better emails beat a long drip: every extra email is another chance to unsubscribe or complain. A brief, the user, or brand memory overrides the default length and timing. Each email gets one job; if two emails have the same job, merge them.

Timing is counted from the trigger (day 0). For B2B audiences, avoid weekend sends. Send at the recipient's local morning where the tool allows it.

## Welcome (newsletter or list signup)

3 emails over about a week. Goal: set expectations and get the first useful action.

| # | Job | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Confirm what they signed up for, how often you'll write, and deliver anything promised | Immediately |
| 2 | The single most useful thing you have for this audience (an issue, a guide, a tool) | Day 2-3 |
| 3 | Introduce the entry offer, softly, with the reason someone like them uses it | Day 5-7 |

Exit: unsubscribes. After email 3, they join the regular newsletter.

## Nurture after a lead magnet

4-5 emails over about two weeks. Goal: turn a download into the entry offer.

| # | Job | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Deliver the lead magnet, and say how to use it in one line | Immediately |
| 2 | Help them use it: one practical step that gets a result from the download | Day 2 |
| 3 | The problem behind the download, in the reader's terms | Day 4-5 |
| 4 | Proof, only if brand memory has some that's cleared to use. With no proof, use how the offer works or who does the work | Day 7-9 |
| 5 | The direct ask: the entry offer, what happens on it, how to book | Day 11-14 |

Exit: takes the entry offer (books, buys, replies), or unsubscribes. Non-converters move to the newsletter, if they agreed to it.

## Onboarding (new customer or user)

3-6 emails over two weeks, driven by behaviour, not the calendar. Goal: reach the first moment of value ("activation").

- Name the activation steps first (for example "calendar connected", "first reminder sent"). Write one email per step, triggered when the step hasn't happened by a set time.
- Branch: anyone who has completed a step skips the emails about it. When the last step is done, end the sequence or switch to a short "what's next" email.
- Offer a human: a reply-to address that a person reads, or a setup call.
- These are service messages tied to what the customer bought. Keep promotion out, or treat the email as marketing (unsubscribe and address required).

## Trial to paid

4-6 emails across the trial. Goal: activation first, then the upgrade.

| # | Job | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Welcome, and the one setup step that shows value fastest | Immediately |
| 2 | Help with that step, or the next one (skip if done) | Day 1-2 |
| 3 | What they can do now that the data or setup is in | Day 3-5 |
| 4 | Proof or a concrete use, only from brand memory | Day 6-8 |
| 5 | The trial is ending: date, what happens next, plans and prices from brand memory | 2-3 days before the end |
| 6 | Last day, or the day after: how to upgrade, what they keep or lose | Last day |

State the true end date and what happens at the end (for example "no card on file, so nothing is charged"). The end of a trial is a real deadline, so it can be named. Exit: upgrades, or the trial ends (move to a short expired-trial win-back only if they agreed to marketing email).

## Re-engagement

3 emails over about two weeks, for people who haven't opened or clicked in a set period (often 60-120 days). Goal: win them back, or let them go.

| # | Job | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Check in: remind them what they signed up for, offer the best recent thing | Day 0 |
| 2 | Ask plainly whether they still want these emails: one click to stay, one to unsubscribe. Offer a lower frequency only if brand memory records one | Day 5-7 |
| 3 | The last email: say plainly that you'll stop emailing unless they click to stay | Day 12-14 |

Sunset rule: anyone who hasn't engaged after email 3 is suppressed (no more marketing email). Say this in email 3, then do it. It protects deliverability, and it's the honest version of "last chance". Don't offer an incentive unless brand memory has one.

Opens are unreliable (privacy features fire them automatically), so judge engagement on clicks and replies where possible.

## Post-purchase

3-4 emails. Goal: get value from the purchase, then a review or the next step.

| # | Job | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Receipt and what happens next (service message) | Immediately |
| 2 | Getting the most from it: one tip | Day 2-5, or after delivery |
| 3 | Check in, and how to get help | Day 7-14 |
| 4 | A review, referral or the next product, only if it fits | Day 14-30 |

## Event follow-up (webinar, workshop, conference)

2-4 emails. Goal: deliver what was promised, then one next step.

- Split attendees from no-shows. Attendees: thanks, the recording or slides, one idea from the session. No-shows: you missed it, here's the recording.
- Check consent per segment. Registering for an event lets you send what they registered for (the link, the recording). Marketing follow-ups need marketing consent. People without it get the recording and nothing else.
- Use only what happened at the event from the notes you're given. Never invent attendance numbers, quotes or poll results.

## Newsletter

One email per issue, on the brand's schedule. Several links are fine, but one primary call to action leads. Keep the brand's email word limit unless brand memory sets a separate one for the newsletter.

## Exit and branch rules that apply to every type

- Everyone exits on unsubscribe, immediately, across every sequence.
- Everyone exits when they do what the sequence asks (book, buy, upgrade). Don't keep selling to someone who already said yes.
- Pause or exit when someone replies, so a person can answer.
- Don't run two marketing sequences at once for the same person. Say which one wins.
- State what happens after the last email (newsletter, suppression, nothing).
