# Lawful basis for outreach

The list summary flags what applies to the countries in the list, in one line each. It doesn't give legal advice or lecture: it says what has to be true before anyone sends, and points to whoever owns compliance when it's unclear.

## By country

| Contacts in | Rule | What has to be true before emailing |
|---|---|---|
| Canada | CASL | Consent. Express (an opt-in you can show), or implied: a purchase or contract in the last two years, an inquiry in the last six months, an address the person published in a business role with no "no unsolicited email" note (and the message fits their role), or an address they gave you directly without saying no. Every email identifies the sender, gives a mailing address and a working unsubscribe, honoured within 10 business days. Keep a record of how consent was obtained. |
| US | CAN-SPAM | No prior consent needed for B2B email, but every email has an honest sender and subject, a physical postal address, and an opt-out honoured within 10 business days. Opt-outs are never sold or shared. |
| UK | UK GDPR + PECR | A lawful basis for holding the contact's data, usually legitimate interests with a short written assessment, and a privacy notice at first contact. PECR allows B2B email to corporate addresses without consent, with an opt-out in every email, but not to sole traders or partnerships. |
| EU | GDPR + national e-privacy law | Same lawful basis and notice as the UK. Email marketing rules vary by country: Germany generally requires prior consent even for B2B email. Check the country before sending. |
| Elsewhere | Local law | Say the country isn't covered here and needs a check. |

## By source of the list

- **CRM export:** check that the CRM records how each contact was obtained and any opt-outs. Contacts with a do-not-contact flag are removed.
- **Event attendee export from the organizer:** attendees gave their details to the organizer, not to the user. Being on the list is not consent to email under CASL, and isn't a lawful basis under GDPR by itself. Check the registration terms (did attendees agree to hear from sponsors or exhibitors?). People who handed over a business card or scanned at the user's booth gave their address directly.
- **Webinar or form registrants for the user's own event:** don't call it consent. Say to check what the form said. Under CASL a registration may count as an inquiry (six months of implied consent) depending on that wording; under GDPR it depends on the notice on the form. It isn't open-ended permission for sales email.
- **Directory or association export:** published business addresses may support implied consent under CASL only if the message relates to the person's role and the listing doesn't say "no unsolicited email". Check the directory's terms of use too.
- **Purchased or rented lists:** consent doesn't transfer by default. Ask the vendor how consent was obtained and get it in writing.
- **URLs only:** there are no contacts yet. Any contact found later needs its own basis.

## Personal and role addresses

- **Personal addresses** (Gmail, Hotmail, ISP addresses) belong to a person, not a role. Never look them up or add them from the web, and never guess them. If one is already in the user's file, flag it as not for cold email without consent.
- **Guessed addresses** (firstname@domain) are invented data. Don't add them.
- **Role addresses** (info@, hello@, sales@) reach a shared inbox. Fine for a first message to a small business under CAN-SPAM; under CASL treat them like any published business address.

## The line in the summary

One line per regime present, plus one line per source that needs a check. For example:

- "Canada (9 accounts): CASL needs consent. The expo attendee list alone isn't consent; check whether attendees agreed to hear from exhibitors, or start with the CRM contacts who have a relationship."
- "US (2 accounts): CAN-SPAM. Include your postal address and an unsubscribe."
- "Germany (1 account): GDPR, and Germany generally requires prior consent for B2B email. Hold this one until that's confirmed."
