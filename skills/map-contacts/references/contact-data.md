# Contact details, staleness and conflicts

## Personal emails and mobile numbers

Never look for, guess or pass on a personal email address or mobile number, even when the user asks, and even when one is already in a source file (an old CRM import, a resume, a scraped list). It's personal data the person didn't give for work. Sending to it, or texting a mobile number, without consent is a compliance risk (anti-spam and texting laws require consent for commercial texts in Canada and the US) and it lands badly with the person.

Decline in one or two sentences, one of which names the consent risk (for example: "I won't source personal emails or mobiles: texting or emailing people there without their agreement breaks anti-spam and texting consent rules."). Then help with what works:
- a work email already on file, with its source
- LinkedIn: a connection request or a message, from whoever has the strongest shared context
- an introduction from someone already engaged (the champion or the person in the trial)
- the company's general contact or main line, for asking who owns the topic
- a work-email guess from the company's pattern, labelled unverified, only if the user wants that

If a source contains a personal address, leave it out of the contacts file and say in Check before outreach that the record holds one and shouldn't be used. Don't repeat the address in the reply.

## Guessing work emails

Only when the user asks. Then:
1. Show the pattern evidence: the addresses on file that follow it (for example "andre@ and nadia@ both use first name @saltboxkitchen.example"). One address isn't a pattern: say the guess is weaker.
2. Write each guess on its own line as `<address> (unverified guess from pattern <pattern>)`, in the reply and in the file.
3. Keep addresses on file and guesses visibly separate.
4. Recommend verifying before any send: a guessed address that bounces hurts the sender's reputation.

No pattern on file? Say so, and offer the route to find one (an email signature, the user's own inbox, a verification tool they already have). Never guess a phone number.

## Staleness

Date every source: the save date on a page, the export date on a file, the call date on notes. Then:
- **Someone left:** a newer source puts them at another company, or says they've gone. Mark `left`, move them to Not contacting, and look for their successor. A past champion who moved is a separate opportunity at the new company: say so in one line.
- **Title out of date:** use the newest dated source. Flag the older title ("CRM still says Assistant Controller, from 2023").
- **Old source:** a page or export over 12 months old makes every title on it uncertain. Say the date and that titles and staff may have changed. Mark contacts known only from it `stale` or confidence low.
- **Records to update:** list what the user should change in their CRM (left, new title, new contact). Don't edit their files.

## Conflicts and wrong-company rows

- Same name, different titles: newest dated source wins; mention the other.
- A row at a similarly named company (Marigold Foods vs Marigold Group), a parent or a subsidiary: exclude it unless a source ties the person to this account, and say who was excluded and why.
- Two sources disagree on who holds a role: present both and say what would settle it.

## Fit

Read `brand/audiences.md` and `BRAND.md` for who the brand is for and not for. If the account's size, region or type falls outside, say it first, recommend confirming fit before any outreach, and still deliver the map so the user can decide. If the account's own words touch the fit question (a question about scale in a webinar), name it.
