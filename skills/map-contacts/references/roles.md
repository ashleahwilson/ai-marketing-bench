## What counts as evidence for each role

A role is a claim about how someone behaves in this deal. Assign it from what a source shows them doing or saying, and use the title only as a starting point.

| Role | Strong evidence | Title-only hint (medium at best) |
|---|---|---|
| **Economic buyer** | A source says they sign, approve or hold the budget ("Rosa signs off anything over $2,000"); a past deal stalled on their budget decision | Owner, CEO, president, CFO, VP of the function that pays |
| **Champion** | They raised the problem, started a trial, asked for a proposal, or said they'd take it to the buyer; they pushed for you before | The manager who owns the problem day to day |
| **User** | A source says they do the work the product changes ("does the POS reconciliations by hand") | Practitioner titles in the affected function |
| **Technical evaluator** | They ran a technical review, own the systems it connects to, or a posting gives that role vendor evaluation | IT, systems, operations or RevOps titles |
| **Blocker** | A source shows resistance or a stake in the status quo: chose or is close to the incumbent, built the current process and defends it, froze the budget | None. Never infer a blocker from a title |
| **Influencer** | Others defer to them, or they shape the requirements without owning the budget or the work | Senior peers in adjacent functions |

Rules:
- **No evidence, no blocker.** If nothing in the sources shows resistance, the blocker is "unknown" and goes in Gaps as a question to answer on the first call.
- **A champion is earned.** If nobody has engaged yet, name the *likely* champion and say the role is unconfirmed until they respond.
- **Ambiguous evidence is a question, not a pick.** When two people could hold one role (a CRO who "owns the number" and a CFO whose team holds the budget), say so and say what would settle it. In Guided mode, ask.
- **One person can hold two roles.** In a small company the owner is often economic buyer and champion.

## Confidence

- **High:** a call, email, reply, CRM activity or org chart shows it.
- **Medium:** a strong title match plus one supporting fact, or an indirect quote from a colleague.
- **Low:** title only, or a source over 12 months old.

## Who to contact first, and in what order

Lead with the person most likely to answer and to carry it inside. In order of preference:
1. **Someone already in motion:** replied, asked a question, started a trial, attended and engaged. Answer them first.
2. **The likely champion:** owns the problem day to day and has some pull with the buyer.
3. **The economic buyer,** a few business days later, from a different angle (risk and money), and never in the same thread. If the champion said they'd take it to the buyer, let them, and hold the direct approach until the champion agrees.
4. **The technical evaluator,** when the champion is engaged or the evaluation starts.
5. **Users,** to build the case from the work itself, usually via the champion.

Blockers aren't cold-contacted first. Plan how they're brought in: usually by the champion, with a reason that serves them. Timing a source gives ("come back in December with a number") overrides this order: put it in the order with its date.

Each line in the order says who, the role, why, and when. Never put someone who left, someone stale, or someone at another company in the order.

## Gaps: roles nobody known fills

For every role with nobody known, write one line: the role, the titles to look for, and where to find them (the company's team page, a LinkedIn search by title, asking the champion "who else would look at this?", a job posting). A vacancy is a gap with a date: a role being hired means nobody fills it yet, and the new hire becomes the person to meet when they start.

## Committee size by company size

A rough guide for how many roles to expect, not a quota:
- Under 20 people: one or two people, usually the owner plus whoever does the work.
- 20 to 200: three to five (buyer, champion, technical evaluator, a user or two).
- Over 200: five or more, often with a finance partner and a security or procurement step.

Don't pad the map to reach these numbers. Missing people are gaps.
